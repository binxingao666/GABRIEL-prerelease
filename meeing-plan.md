**1. Research question and why it is interesting**

**Core question**
How do entrepreneurial teams **construct, revise, and (sometimes) converge on shared theories of value over time**, and how does that trajectory relate to:

-   Individual member experiences (involvement, satisfaction, willingness to stay on the team)
-   Team level functioning (coordination, conflict, procedural justice, shared representation)
-   Venture progress and performance (quality of experimentation, coherence of the business model, pivot, termination)

**Connection to the theory-based view (TBV)**

In the TBV, firms are seen as sets of theories about how value is created and captured. These theories guide experimentation and learning, but most work is at the firm or founder level and is cross sectional or static. There is very little empirical work that:

-   Observes how multiple individuals within a team each hold their own theory of value,
-   Tracks how those individual theories are aggregated, negotiated, and revised into a shared representation, and
-   Links that joint evolution to subsequent experimentation and venture performance.

**Connection to the teams and shared representation literatures**

The teams literature has rich work on shared mental models, leadership, and procedural justice, but:

-   Very few studies observe the content of a shared representation in detail.
-   Almost none trace fine grained trajectories of how shared representations emerge and change over time in real work.
-   Work on leadership and facilitation rarely ties leadership processes to the quality and evolution of a shared theory, or to the trade off between “happy teams” and “strong, coherent venture ideas” that may require tough exclusion of some ideas.

Our setting gives us a rare opportunity to study the emergence and evolution of a shared representation in vivo, in entrepreneurial teams that are working on consequential projects, with repeated, structured documentation.

**2. Field setting and overall design**

·   We are considering implementing a study that will take the form of a field experiment in the UMD setting. Given how central the aggregation process is for us – from individual theories of value to a collective, shared theory – **we want to observe that process for all groups, in this and in subsequent iterations.** Concretely, our plan is all groups will always be asked to submit individual theories of value before coming to class, and then, after in class discussion, the team will submit a collective theory of value. This will be done multiple times (e.g. when they have to submit hps, when they have to decide on the tests, when they interpret the results). At this stage, our primary theoretical interest is in the role of a leader who is not a leader in terms of power, but in terms of coordination and facilitation of this aggregation process.

·   Some of the dynamics we anticipate is that some team members will see their ideas more recognized than others, and this will affect their emotional reactions, their willingness to stay in the team, their willingness to take on tasks. 

·   However, in the UMD context this will not be a randomised controlled trial. We want students to self select into teams, and we also cannot randomly allocate students to sections. This means that we will not be able to fully disentangle any treatment effect from instructor effects and from heterogeneity across groups.

·   Given that constraint, our thinking is the following. The main goal of this first iteration is to learn how to measure and code the relevant constructs: in particular, to develop and test the management report structure, the individual submissions, and the short surveys. At the same time, **we would like to embed some exogenous variation** so that, if the data quality is high, this first wave can already support a clean empirical study.

·   To do that, **our current idea is to ask all teams in all sections to have a facilitator, and to suggest a facilitator at random within each team**. If some teams feel strongly about changing that person, we will allow them to do so, but the initial random assignment still gives us exploitable variation. For example, by chance some of these initially assigned facilitators will be women versus men, or will differ in background and prior experience. The priority for this semester remains to get the measurement vehicle right and to keep student burden low, but in this way, while we are learning and piloting, we still preserve the possibility of a relatively clean study. For example we might see that some groups with a certain type of facilitator will end up being more inclusive in the collective aggregation of their theory of value and maybe this will be positive for affect but detrimental for the success of the venture.

·   Looking forward, once we are ready for a more stringent RCT design, we can be more sophisticated about the facilitation intervention itself. For example, we could compare chosen leaders versus randomly assigned leaders, or we could provide a brief facilitation / coordination training to some leaders but not to others, and then evaluate how those manipulations influence the evolution of theories of value and team dynamics.



**3. Data we plan to collect**

We envisage three main data sources, all aligned with the teaching model so that they are useful to both students and instructors.

**3.1 Individual and shared theories of value over time**

At several key moments, each team member will submit **an individual theory of value**, and the team will then agree on and submit **a shared theory of value**. For example:

-   **Time 0 (after team formation, once they have chosen an idea)**
    -   Each individual submits:
        -   A **diagrammatic representation** (boxes and arrows) and
        -   A short **text explanation**.
    -   The team then meets and submits:
        -   A **shared theory of value** (diagram plus short text) that they all endorse as “our current best collective view”.
-   **Subsequent key milestones** (for example: after initial hypothesis generation, after first round of tests, after a major pivot):
    -   Each individual again submits:
        -   Their **current personal theory of value** given what has been learned.
    -   The team again submits:
        -   An updated **shared theory of value** for that moment in time.

These can be scheduled at fixed weeks (for example week 3, week 6, week 9, week 12), recognising that teams will move at different speeds but that we need common measurement points.

**3.2 Structured management reports (team level, weekly or bi-weekly)**

We will converge on a **standardised management report template** that serves three functions:

1.  Useful to teams for structuring their own work and reflection
2.  Useful to instructors for monitoring and feedback
3.  Useful to researchers for data extraction, with low response burden

The template might include sections such as (I will follow up with another email explicitly on this):

-   **Shared theory of value (snapshot for this week)**
    -   Diagram and short text
-   **Task allocation and contributions**
    -   Who did what, and how time and responsibility were distributed.
-   **Problems, bottlenecks, and coordination issues**

These reports will be our main **team level process trace**.

In addition, we are considering a short **video log** assignment at one or two key points in the semester, in which teams briefly reflect on **how their collaboration is working**. 

We would then use LLMs to transcribe and analyse these video logs, coding for indicators of **affect** (for example enthusiasm, frustration, disengagement) and ***\*procedural justice\**** (for example whether people describe the process of theory aggregation as fair, inclusive, and transparent). This would give us a complementary, more naturalistic window into team dynamics that we can triangulate with the survey measures and the management reports, again with minimal additional burden on students.

**4. Planned measures**

**4.1. Theory of value: content and aggregation**

We would add a section in the MR where students must provide a graphical representation of their theory of value and an accompanying text. *(Individual theories themselves are separate submissions, but the **aggregation outcome** and how it is justified can be seen in the management report.)*

From this we hope to extract::

-   **Presence of an articulated team theory of value** in each period.
-   **Clarity and coherence** of the team theory of value (for example how clearly the cause–impact–value chain is specified).
-   **Degree of convergence** among team members’ ideas, as inferred from:
    -   How many distinct ideas are incorporated into the shared theory.
    -   Whether the shared theory looks like a “Frankenstein” that tries to include every suggestion versus a more selective, focused representation.
-   **Frequency and magnitude of revisions** to the team theory of value over time:
    -   Number of changes in assumptions, customer segment, value proposition, mechanism, and so on.
-   **Link between experiments and theory revisions**:
    -   Whether reported changes in the theory of value are explicitly justified by prior tests or data described in the same or previous reports.

**4.****2. Task allocation and division of labour**

From sections on tasks, priorities, and “who is doing what”:

-   **Who is assigned to which tasks** in each period (task allocation matrix).
-   **Concentration versus dispersion of tasks**:
    -   How much work is concentrated on one or two people versus distributed across team members.
-   **Role specialisation over time**:
    -   Whether particular members consistently handle certain types of tasks (for example customer interviews, analysis, coordination).
-   **Changes in team composition or role**:
    -   Entry or exit of team members noted in reports.
    -   Shifts in who appears as responsible for high priority tasks.

**4.3. Facilitation, coordination, and leadership in practice**

From how teams describe their process, decisions, and responsibilities in the video log:

-   **Evidence of a de facto facilitator or coordinator**, regardless of formal assignment:
    -   One person repeatedly summarising decisions, setting priorities, or assigning tasks in the report.
-   **Degree of centralisation of coordination**:
    -   Whether coordination activities (planning, agenda setting, integrating inputs) are done by one person or rotated / shared across members.
-   **Stability versus rotation of facilitation**:
    -   Does the same person appear as the main coordinator over many reports, or does that role change over time.
-   **Coherence:** 
    -   Extent to which the tasks are coherent with the theory of value

**4.****4. Decision making and use of individual ideas**

-   **Number and type of “critical decisions”** recorded in each period.
-   **Selectivity versus inclusion**:
    -   Whether decisions systematically narrow down options or try to accommodate many conflicting ideas.
-   **Consistency between decisions and stated theory of value**:
    -   Do chosen actions align with the articulated theory, or do reports show drift.

**4.5. Experimentation, hypotheses, and learning** **** **@Rob and Alisha: can we incorporate as a section into MR? if so we could extract:**

From sections on tests, hypotheses, results, and interpretation:

-   **Number of hypotheses formulated** per period.
-   **Number and type of experiments run**:
    -   Customer interviews, A/B tests, prototypes, and so on.
-   **Quality of the hypothesis–test–result–interpretation chain**:
    -   Are hypotheses explicit?
    -   Are they coherent with the theory of value
-   **Speed of learning cycle**:
    -   How often teams move from hypothesis to test to interpretation between reports.

**4.****6. Problems, conflict, and bottlenecks**

From the “problems” and “bottlenecks” sections:

-   **Frequency of reported problems** per period.
-   **Type of problems**:
    -   Technical or market related versus team process issues (coordination, conflict, free riding).
-   **Evidence of disagreements about direction**:
    -   Explicit mention of conflicting views about the theory of value or priorities.
-   **Persistence of unresolved issues**:
    -   Problems that recur across multiple reports without clear resolution.
-   **Ethical or fairness related issues**, if they appear:
    -   For example, perceptions that tasks or recognition are not distributed fairly.

**4.****7. Engagement and involvement** 

From who appears in the reports and how:

-   **Visibility of each team member’s contributions**:
    -   Frequency of name mentions in “who did what” and “next steps”.
-   **Changes in contribution patterns**:
    -   Declining appearance of a member’s name over time (possible disengagement).
-   **Balance between cognitive contributions and execution**:
    -   Whether some members are only listed in low level tasks versus in problem framing, theory construction, and decision making.

**4**.**8. Venture trajectory measures**

Combining the above across weeks:

-   **Number of major pivots** in the theory of value across the semester.
-   **Other performance measure.**
